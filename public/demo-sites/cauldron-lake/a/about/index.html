The story

One person, a couple of printers, and a lot of late nights by the lake.

This is sample text. Replace it with your own story.

Cauldron Lake started the way most workshops do: with one machine on a desk and a list of things that didn't exist yet. The name comes from the kind of place I like best. A caldera is a volcano that has gone quiet and filled with water. It looks still from the shore, but the heat is still down there.

Everything here is printed in layers, a fraction of a millimetre at a time, then cleaned up, sanded and sometimes painted by hand. Some pieces are made in small runs. Others are one of one, made for a single person.

How I work

I design most pieces myself, print them on machines I've tuned for the job, and finish every one by hand. If something isn't right, it gets reprinted. The failures go in a box on the shelf, and a few of them end up in the Workshop.

Commissions

If you have something in mind, a replacement part, a model of somewhere that matters to you, or an idea that only exists as a sketch, write to me. I'll reply with a quote and an honest timeline.
